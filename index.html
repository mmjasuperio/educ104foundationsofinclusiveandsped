Teach the learner, not the label

    An interactive lesson on five areas of special educational need: ADHD, emotional and behavioral disability, specific learning disability, intellectual and developmental disabilities, and speech and language disorders. Work through the six parts in order, or jump to any part from the bar above.

    Categories and terms in special education vary across countries and school systems. This lesson is for educational planning, not clinical diagnosis.

    Myth or fact?

    Start by testing what you already believe. Decide whether each statement is a myth or a fact.

    Explore the five areas

    Choose an area, then open each heading to read about it. Pay closest attention to the classroom interventions; you will use them in the scenarios.

    What might you be seeing?

    Each card describes something a teacher might observe. Select a card, then select the area it most closely matches.

    Real learners often show overlapping characteristics, and one observation is never enough to identify a need. This activity practises recognising typical patterns; identification always comes from proper assessment.

    Classroom scenarios

    Read each situation and choose the response that best fits the interventions in the notes. Every option gives feedback, so it is worth reading why the other choices fall short.

    Check your understanding

    Nine questions covering all five areas. Your score appears at the end.

    Do not teach the disability; teach the learner.

    Two learners with the same disability may have very different strengths, needs, interests, communication styles and support requirements. Base interventions on individual assessment, strengths, functional needs and the learner's educational goals, not on the label alone.
